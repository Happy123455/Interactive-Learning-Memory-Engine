Architectural Space-Making Theory

1. Space as a Medium

Space is not merely vacuum or void; it is the physical medium of architecture. Architectural elements like columns, walls, ceilings, and planes define coordinates, boundaries, and regions of space, making otherwise empty volume readable.

2. Solid & Void (Mass-Space Relationship)

The dialectic of Mass and Void is fundamental to composition. Solids act as positive physical structures, while Voids (courtyards, lightwells, rooms) constitute subtractive negative spaces that generate functional volume, light passage, and perceptual scale.

3. Light & Shadow (Phenomenological Space)

Without light, geometric form remains hidden. Light reveals the texture of boundaries, depth of planes, and creates dynamic temporal boundaries via cast shadows. Shadows offer relief, define spatial thresholds, and establish emotional scale.

4. Scale: Human vs. Architectural

Human scale anchors design to ergonomics, proportion, and active physical contact (indicated by furniture layout). Architectural scale links space to structural engineering, structural spacing, and external dimension patterns.
