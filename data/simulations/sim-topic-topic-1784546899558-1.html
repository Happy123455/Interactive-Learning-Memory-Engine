IS 800:2007 Structural Engineer's Guide

Section 10: Limit State of Connections (Design of Bolts)

1. Nominal Shear Strength of a Bolt ($V_{nsb}$)

Clause 10.3.2: The nominal shear capacity of a bolt is formulated by checking the ultimate tensile strength across the failure/shear planes.

V_nsb = ( f_ub / √3 ) × [ n_n × A_nb + n_s × A_sb ]
  • f_ub: Ultimate tensile strength of the bolt.
  • A_nb: Net tensile shear area of the bolt at the threads (~0.78 × π/4 × d²).
  • n_n: Number of shear planes passing through thread section.

2. Nominal Bearing Strength of a Bolt ($V_{npb}$)

Clause 10.3.4: Under high tension, the bolt body pushes or shears against the edge surface of the plates.

V_npb = 2.5 × k_b × d × t × f_u
  • k_b: Bearing coefficient based on plate boundary conditions.
  • d: Nominal bolt diameter.
  • t: Thickness of thinner connected plate.
  • f_u: Ultimate tensile strength of the plate material.

🧸 THE GUMMY BEAR METAPHOR (ELI5)

Think of a metal bolt as a dense **Gummy Bear** pinned inside two cookies (the metal plates). If you pull the top cookie right, and the bottom cookie left, the gummy bear gets squished inside!

If the gummy is too soft, it slices in half (Shear Failure). If the cookie is too crumbly, the gummy bear rips right through the edge of the cookie (Bearing Failure).
