IS 456:2000 - Plain and Reinforced Concrete - Code of Practice

Clause 5.2.2.1 - Limit State Method vs. Working Stress Method

The Code recognizes two methods of design for reinforced concrete structures:

  • Limit State Method (LSM): This method considers the structure at various limit states (e.g., collapse, serviceability) under characteristic loads and material strengths, using partial safety factors. It aims for a more realistic and economical design, utilizing material strength more effectively.
  • Working Stress Method (WSM): This method designs the structure to remain elastic under service loads, with stresses kept below permissible limits. It tends to be more conservative, leading to heavier and less economical sections due to its simpler, linear elastic assumptions and higher factors of safety on stresses.

5.2.2.1 Limit State Method

This method shall be used for general design of reinforced concrete structures. It is based on the principle that the structure must satisfy all applicable limit states at ultimate and serviceability conditions.

5.2.2.2 Working Stress Method

This method may be used in circumstances where limit state method cannot be conveniently applied, or where specified by the client or regulatory authority. However, it generally leads to uneconomical sections compared to the limit state method.

Note: Partial safety factors for loads and materials are fundamental to the Limit State Method to account for uncertainties in design parameters.
