Dynamic Limit State Mathematical Model

The ultimate moment of resistance of a doubly reinforced rectangular beam section is computed by summing the contributions of the concrete block in compression, compression steel ($A_{sc}$), and tension steel ($A_{st}$):

$$M_u = 0.36 f_{ck} b x_u (d - 0.42 x_u) + (f_{sc} - f_{cc}) A_{sc} (d - d')$$
1. Concrete Block Contribution ($M_{u1}$): $$M_{u1} = 0.36 f_{ck} b x_u (d - 0.42 x_u)$$ Represents standard compressive concrete wedge capacity.
2. Compression Steel Contribution ($M_{u2}$): $$M_{u2} = (f_{sc} - f_{cc}) A_{sc} (d - d')$$ Represents the auxiliary steel couple working to boost structural strength.

Doubly Reinforced Beams Guide

When a beam section is subjected to extremely high bending moments, expanding the depth of the beam may be restricted due to headroom clearances or architectural aesthetics. In such architectural constraints, simply increasing tension reinforcement is not viable as it leads to a dangerous, non-ductile over-reinforced failure.

The Solution: Add compression reinforcement ($A_{sc}$) in the compression zone. This steel shares the extreme compressive load, ensuring structural stability without modifying the physical beam boundary dimensions.

Key Steps of Design Evaluation:

  1. Compute reinforcement areas $A_{st}$ and $A_{sc}$ based on selections.
  2. Solve the exact non-linear strain compatibility to find the Neutral Axis ($x_u$).
  3. Compute the strain $\varepsilon_{sc}$ in the compression reinforcement layer to derive its stress value $f_{sc}$.
  4. Determine if the beam is under-reinforced ($x_u < x_{u,max}$) or over-reinforced ($x_u > x_{u,max}$).
  5. Calculate the ultimate Moment of Resistance ($M_u$) according to IS 456 Code directives.
