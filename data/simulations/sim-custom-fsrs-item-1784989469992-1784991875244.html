IS 456 Code Constant Demystifier Cheat Sheet

1. Why $0.36 f_{ck} b X_u$?

It is the area of the parabolic-rectangular concrete stress block. Parabolic from neutral axis up to $0.58 X_u$, then rectangular ($0.446 f_{ck}$) for top $0.42 X_u$. Integrating gives exactly 0.36!

2. Why $0.446 f_{ck}$?

Characteristics cube strength is $f_{ck}$. Structural concrete strength is taken as $0.67 f_{ck}$ (accounting for shape/size effects). Dividing by partial safety factor $\gamma_c = 1.5$ gives $\frac{0.67}{1.5} = \mathbf{0.446 f_{ck}}$.

3. Why $0.87 f_y$?

Design yield stress of steel reinforcement. Yield strength $f_y$ divided by partial safety factor for steel $\gamma_s = 1.15 \implies \frac{1}{1.15} = \mathbf{0.87 f_y}$.

4. Where do $0.53d, 0.48d, 0.46d$ ($X_{u,\max}$) come from?

From linear strain strain compatibility! $\frac{X_{u,\max}}{d} = \frac{0.0035}{0.0035 + (0.002 + \frac{0.87 f_y}{E_s})}$. Substituting steel elastic modulus $E_s = 2 \times 10^5$ N/mm²:
• Fe 250 $\rightarrow 0.531 d \approx \mathbf{0.53d}$
• Fe 415 $\rightarrow 0.479 d \approx \mathbf{0.48d}$
• Fe 500 $\rightarrow 0.456 d \approx \mathbf{0.46d}$

5. Why $(f_{sc} - f_{cc}) A_{sc}$?

Compression steel occupies area $A_{sc}$ in the concrete compression zone. Since concrete force was already calculated over the full area $b X_u$, we must subtract concrete stress ($f_{cc} \approx 0.446 f_{ck}$) from steel stress ($f_{sc}$) to avoid double counting!
