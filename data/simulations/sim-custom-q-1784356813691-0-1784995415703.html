Simple Playground Explanation

How Beams Work Like a Playground Seesaw! 🎢

Forget hard college formulas for a minute. Let's see how concrete, steel bars, and T-shapes act like sponges and rubber bands!

1

The Sponge & The Rubber Band (Compression vs Tension)

Imagine holding a long rectangular kitchen sponge and bending it downwards:

  • Top Part gets SQUISHED: This is called COMPRESSION. Concrete loves being squished!
  • Bottom Part gets PULLED: This is called TENSION. Concrete snaps easily when pulled!

💡 The Trick: We put strong steel rods at the bottom because steel acts like giant unbreakable rubber bands!

➡️ PUSHSQUISH ZONEPUSH ⬅️
⬅️ PULLSTEEL RODSPULL ➡️

Concrete handles top push, Steel handles bottom pull!

2

Why a "T" Shape Table?

Why not just make a big rectangle beam? Because concrete is heavy and expensive!

Top Table (Flange)

Since the top part gets squished hard, we make it wide ($1000\text{ mm}$) to spread out the heavy push force!

Vertical Stem (Web)

The bottom concrete doesn't help with tension, so we trim it skinny ($b_w$) just to hold the steel bars in place!

3

The Neutral Line ($x_u$): The Peace Line 🕊️

In the middle of the beam, there is an imaginary magic line where the concrete is neither pushed nor pulled. That line is called the Neutral Axis ($x_u$).

Equalizing Tug-of-War:
$$\text{Total Concrete Squish Force } (C) = \text{Total Steel Pull Force } (T)$$
$0.36 \cdot f_{ck} \cdot b_f \cdot x_u = 0.87 \cdot f_y \cdot A_{st}$
In our question: $x_u = 98.46\text{ mm}$. Since the top table (flange) is $125\text{ mm}$ thick, $98.46\text{ mm}$ stays inside the top table!
4

The Bending Strength ($M_u$)

Think of strength like opening a heavy door. The further away you push from the hinge, the easier it opens!

Lever Arm ($z$)

$d - 0.42 x_u$

Distance between top compression center and bottom steel pull!

Moment Capacity ($M_u$)

$\text{Force} \times \text{Lever Arm}$

Maximum bending load the beam can carry before snapping!
