Aggregate Characterization Tests

Aggregate occupies up to 70-80% of concrete volume and 90% of asphalt mix. For structural safety and durable roads, aggregates must endure crushing, impacts, continuous abrasive friction, and fluctuating environmental exposure. Engineers run specialized mechanical tests to qualify these geological specimens.

Dynamic Loading

1. Aggregate Impact Test

Principle: Measures "toughness" (resistance to sudden shock loading). A standard 13.5 - 14 kg hammer is allowed to fall freely from a height of 380 mm for 15 blows onto a cup filled with aggregates.

Uses: Critically determines aggregate suitability for wearing surfaces of pavements (such as roads and runways) where fast-moving wheel loads impact aggregates directly.

Static Loading

2. Aggregate Crushing Test

Principle: Measures resistance to crushing under a gradually applied compressive load. Aggregates are subjected to a steady load of 40 tons (400 kN) applied via a compression machine over 10 minutes.

Uses: Determines crushing behavior under heavy stationary structures or high-density static loads. Standard maximum crushing limit for wearing surfaces is 30%, and 45% for general concrete.

Frictional Wear

3. Los Angeles Abrasion Test

Principle: Measures structural hardness (resistance to wear). Aggregates are rotated inside a steel hollow drum along with steel spheres (abrasive charge) at 30-33 RPM for 500 or 1000 revolutions.

Uses: Simulates the continuous scuffing, grinding, and scraping of heavy traffic and pneumatic tires on aggregates, especially for highway base course layers.

Weathering

4. Durability / Soundness Test

Principle: Evaluates resistance to expansion and disintegration caused by weather forces. Samples are immersed alternately in saturated solutions of sodium sulfate (Na₂SO₄) or magnesium sulfate (MgSO₄) followed by oven drying.

Uses: Crucial for projects in freeze-thaw climates, coastal areas, or damp environments where hydration or ice crystal expansion can split the concrete framework.

Particle Shape

5. Shape & Texture Tests

Principle: Uses metal gauges (thickness gauge for Flakiness and length gauge for Elongation) to find the weight percentage of excessively thin (flaky) or overly long (elongated) aggregate particles.

Uses: Prevents poor concrete workability. Excessively flaky/elongated particles have high surface areas, demanding more water/cement paste, and break easily under structural stresses.

Porosity & Density

6. Specific Gravity & Absorption

Principle: Measures mass density of aggregate particles relative to pure water, and calculates internal water-permeable void percentages by weighing specimens in dry, submerged, and saturated surface-dry states.

Uses: Vital for calculating precise concrete mix designs, adjusting water-to-cement ratios dynamically based on raw moisture content, and determining aggregate strength indicators.
