Hydrological Pan Evaporation Theory

Pan Evaporation calculations serve as direct physical estimators of atmospheric vapor demand. They assist in quantifying evaporation rates across agricultural reservoirs and massive open waters.

The Dalton Equation mathematically states that the rate of evaporation is directly proportional to the difference in vapor pressure between the water surface and the surrounding air atmosphere:

Ep = (a + b · u) (es - ea)

Where es represents the saturation vapor pressure computed directly from thermal metrics, and ea is actual vapor pressure representing ambient atmospheric moisture levels. The coefficient u denotes horizontal wind speed.

The Pan Coefficient ($C_p$) compensates for the structural differences between a shallow, heat-conductive metallic pan and a natural, deep reservoir water body. This is crucial for converting $E_p$ to actual reservoir evaporation ($E$):

E = Cp × Ep

Different geometric installations utilize varying standardized structural coefficients:
• US Weather Bureau Class A Pan: $C_p = 0.70$
• Indian Standard Pan (IS:5973): $C_p = 0.80$
• Colorado Sunken Pan: $C_p = 0.78$
