# MATHEMATICAL MODEL & PHYSICAL ENGINE

The underlying simulation engine must calculate the shear capacity of a reinforced concrete beam in strict accordance with **IS 456:2000**. Provide continuous real-time recalculations as parameters change via sliders or pointer drag actions.

## Geometry and Material Properties
*   Overall Width ($b$): Fixed at $300\text{ mm}$
*   Overall Depth ($D$): Fixed at $500\text{ mm}$
*   Effective Cover ($c$): $30\text{ mm}$
*   Effective Depth ($d$): $D - c = 470\text{ mm}$
*   Main Tension Reinforcement ($A_{st}$): 4 bars of $20\text{ mm}$ diameter.
    $$A_{st} = 4 \times \frac{\pi}{4} \times 20^2 = 1256.64\text{ mm}^2$$
*   Percentage Tension Steel ($p_t$):
    $$p_t = \frac{100 \times A_{st}}{b \times d} = \frac{100 \times 1256.64}{300 \times 470} \approx 0.891\%$$
*   Concrete Grade: M-20 ($f_{ck} = 20\text{ N/mm}^2$)
*   Stirrup Yield Strength ($f_{yv}$): Fe-250 ($f_y = 250\text{ N/mm}^2$)
*   Stirrup Diameter ($d_v$): $8\text{ mm}$ (Two-legged, $n = 2$)
*   Area of Shear Reinforcement ($A_{sv}$):
    $$A_{sv} = 2 \times \frac{\pi}{4} \times 8^2 \approx 100.53\text{ mm}^2$$

## IS 456 Shear Calculations
1.  **Nominal Shear Stress ($\tau_v$)**:
    $$\tau_v = \frac{V_u}{b \times d}$$
    where $V_u$ is the user-controlled external Shear Force (ranging dynamically from $0\text{ kN}$ to $350\text{ kN}$ via sliders or gesture inputs).
2.  **Design Shear Strength of Concrete ($\tau_c$)**:
    According to IS 456:2000 Table 19, for $M20$ concrete and $p_t = 0.891\%$, interpolate $\tau_c$:
    *   For $p_t = 0.75\%$, $\tau_c = 0.56\text{ N/mm}^2$
    *   For $p_t = 1.00\%$, $\tau_c = 0.62\text{ N/mm}^2$
    *   Interpolated $\tau_c \approx 0.56 + \frac{0.891 - 0.75}{1.00 - 0.75} \times (0.62 - 0.56) \approx 0.594\text{ N/mm}^2$.
3.  **Maximum Shear Stress ($\tau_{c,\text{max}}$)**:
    For M20 concrete, $\tau_{c,\text{max}} = 2.8\text{ N/mm}^2$.
4.  **Failure Regime Decision Tree**:
    *   **Case A: Redesign Section Required**
        If $\tau_v > \tau_{c,\text{max}}$, the concrete itself will crush regardless of stirrups. *Trigger Alarm Tone, Red Screen Flash, and Voice Alert: "Oh no! The scissor action is too strong! The cookie dough is crushing! We must make the beam bigger!"*
    *   **Case B: Nominal/Minimum Stirrups Required**
        If $\tau_v \le \tau_c$, concrete alone can theoretically withstand the shear, but minimum shear reinforcement is mandatory under Clause 26.5.1.6:
        $$s_{v,\text{max}} = \frac{0.87 \times f_{yv} \times A_{sv}}{0.4 \times b} = \frac{0.87 \times 250 \times 100.53}{0.4 \times 300} \approx 182.2\text{ mm}$$
    *   **Case C: Design Stirrups Required**
        If $\tau_c < \tau_v \le \tau_{c,\text{max}}$, design shear reinforcement to carry the excess shear $V_{us}$:
        $$V_{us} = V_u - \tau_c \times b \times d$$
        $$s_v = \frac{0.87 \times f_{yv} \times A_{sv} \times d}{V_{us}}$$
        The spacing $s_v$ must not exceed the limits of Clause 26.5.1.5:
        $$s_{v,\text{max}} = \min(0.75 \times d, 300\text{ mm}, \text{Calculated Spacing})$$
        Where $0.75 \times d = 0.75 \times 470 = 352.5\text{ mm}$. Hence, upper bound is capped at $300\text{ mm}$.
