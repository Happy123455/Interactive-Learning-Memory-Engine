Hydrology Secrets: Page 7 - Precipitation Forms & Types

Rain: It's when warm air makes cloud-sponges squeeze out water, and it's not too cold down below!
Drizzle: Just like rain, but super tiny drops that gently mist down from thin clouds. Still needs warmish air!
Snow: Wow, fluffy snow! When the sky gets super chilly (below freezing!) all the way from the cloud to the ground, water turns into tiny ice fairies that dance down! Needs cold air and ice crystals.
Sleet: Imagine tiny ice pellets! This happens when rain falls from a warm cloud, but then freezes into little ice balls as it passes through a really cold layer of air close to the ground.
Hail: Uh oh, bouncy hail! That's when ice goes on a rollercoaster ride inside a super stormy cloud, getting bigger and bigger with strong winds pushing it up, before it drops as hard ice chunks! Needs super cold air, ice crystals, and strong updrafts.
Freezing Rain: Tricky stuff! Rain falls from a warm cloud, passes through a cold layer, but doesn't quite freeze into sleet. Instead, it gets supercooled and turns into ice *only when it hits* cold surfaces on the ground!
