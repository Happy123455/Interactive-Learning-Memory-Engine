Unit 01 Elements of Space Making

Space making begins with the placement of three-dimensional geometric form. These forms define boundaries and visual physical limitations. Primary volumes (Cube, Cylinder, Sphere) establish the baseline visual mass and scale of structural envelopes.

The translation of simple geometry into tectonic experiences relies on two primary principles:

  • Scale: Relation of architectural space dimensions to human proportions (Anthropometrics).
  • Proportion: Dynamic mathematical ratios governing spatial layout, width, length, and height.

Ratio Rules Golden Section & Harmony

Architects from Antiquity, the Renaissance (Alberti, Palladio) to Modernism (Le Corbusier's Modulor) look for structural perfection in proportions.

Ratio = Height / Width

Ratios between 1.2 and 1.5 represent balanced, human-friendly spatial transitions. Excessively tall ratios can induce claustrophobia, creating the "Tunnel Effect" (> 1.5), while extremely flat ratios appear static, squashed, or expansive.

Vastu & Geometry

The Vastu Purusha Mandala represents an ancient spatial grid methodology, using subdivisions ($9\times9$ or $3\times3$) to coordinate geographic and energetic layouts, ensuring spatial resonance with cosmic geometries.
