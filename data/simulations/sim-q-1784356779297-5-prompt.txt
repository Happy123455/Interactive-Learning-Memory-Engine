### Blueprint: Interactive Groove Weld Capacity Sandbox

**Objective:** Build a gamified structural simulation where users analyze groove weld strength through drag-and-drop mechanics.

**1. Architecture & UI:**
*   **Viewport:** 100vh, full-screen "Cyber-Engineering" dashboard. Use CSS Grid/Flexbox with glassmorphism panels (translucent dark backgrounds, cyan/neon-purple accents).
*   **Canvas Engine:** HTML5 Canvas for real-time drafting. Use `requestAnimationFrame` for fluid animations.
*   **Physics/Math:** Implement JS logic to calculate weld capacity ($T_d = f_y / (\sqrt{3} \cdot \gamma_{mw}) \cdot l_w \cdot t_e$) where variables are dynamically injected.

**2. Simulation Workflow:**
*   **Stage 1: Identification (ELI5 Mode):** Present the problem as a "Plate Puzzle." Use clickable text fields for $a$ (thickness), $b$ (tensile force), $c$ (length), and $d$ (weld type).
*   **Stage 2: Interactive Drafting:** When a variable is selected, animate a 2D cross-section growth (e.g., the plate thickens while a "glow" effect highlights the effective throat). Use `window.speechSynthesis` to explain: *"Think of the weld like a super-strong glue! If the plate is thick, we need more glue to hold it tight."*
*   **Stage 3: Drag-and-Drop Formulas:** Render "Value Cards" (e.g., 410 MPa, $L=200mm$). User must drag these onto specific slots in the equation.
    *   **Success:** Play a bright synth chime, show a green pulse, and trigger a formula visual.
    *   **Failure:** Play a low-pitch "thunk" and show a red ring.
*   **Stage 4: Gamified Analysis:** Integrate a "Stress-Meter" slider. As the user slides force upward, increase the pitch of a Web Audio Oscillator (100Hz to 800Hz) to simulate tension. At capacity, trigger a particle animation "pop" of the weld joint.

**3. Engineering Requirements:**
*   **IS 456/800 References:** Include a clickable "Virtual Codebook" icon. Clicking it opens a side panel overlay showing Clause 10.5.
*   **Visuals:** Use `CanvasRenderingContext2D` to draw clean force vectors (red arrows) that scale in length relative to the input load. 
*   **Gestures:** Enable mouse/pointer tracking; moving the mouse across the joint should rotate the 2D view and deform the geometry slightly to visualize "structural strain."
