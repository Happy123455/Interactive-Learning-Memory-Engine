📑 Structural Engineering Manual

Shear Lag refers to the non-uniform tension distribution across a member's cross-section when some elements of the cross-section are not directly connected.


In our steel angle profile, the leg that is connected directly to the gusset plate experiences immediate stress. However, stress must travel through shear deformation to reach the outstanding leg, causing a lazy wave (lag effect) and reducing performance.


By connecting a Lug Angle (a helper bracket) to the outstanding leg, we engage both legs of the main angle rapidly. This distributes forces evenly, mitigates eccentric bending, and significantly improves cross-sectional efficiency U.


Formula: U = 1 - (x̄ / L) where x̄ is connection eccentricity and L is connection length. A longer connection allows the outstanding leg to catch up naturally!
