1. Depth of Neutral Axis ($x_u$) Derivation

By equating total compressive force in concrete ($C$) to total tensile force in steel ($T$):

Total Compressive Force ($C$) = $0.36 \cdot f_{ck} \cdot b \cdot x_u$

Total Tensile Force ($T$) = $0.87 \cdot f_y \cdot A_{st}$

At Equilibrium ($C = T$):

0.36 · fck · b · xu = 0.87 · fy · Ast

⇒ xu = (0.87 · fy · Ast) / (0.36 · fck · b)

Live Substitution: xu = (0.87 × 415 × 942) / (0.36 × 25 × 250) = 151.8 mm

2. Ultimate Moment of Resistance ($M_u$) & Lever Arm ($z$)

The lever arm ($z$) is the distance between the line of action of compressive force $C$ (acting at $0.42 x_u$ from top) and tensile force $T$:

Lever Arm ($z$) = $d - 0.42 \cdot x_u$

Moment of Resistance ($M_u = C \cdot z$ or $T \cdot z$):

Mu = 0.36 · fck · b · xu · (d - 0.42 · xu)

OR Mu = 0.87 · fy · Ast · (d - 0.42 · xu)

🧸 The Story of Mr. Concrete & Captain Steel

Imagine a bridge or ceiling beam is like a playground seesaw made of concrete and steel bars. When heavy trucks drive over it, the beam wants to bend downwards into a smile shape 🙂!

🧱

1. Top Squish Zone

The top part gets squeezed hard! Concrete is a giant stone monster that LOVES being squished ($C = 0.36 f_{ck} b x_u$).

⛓️

2. Bottom Stretch Zone

The bottom part gets pulled apart! Concrete cracks easily when pulled, so we put Captain Steel bars at the bottom to pull back ($T = 0.87 f_y A_{st}$).

🎯

3. The Neutral Line ($x_u$)

In the exact middle line, there is NO squishing and NO pulling. That line is called the Neutral Axis ($x_u$)!

⚠️ Why Over-reinforcing is SCARY:

If you put TOO MANY steel bars at the bottom, the steel will never stretch, but the concrete top will suddenly crush and shatter like glass with NO warning! That's why engineers design Under-Reinforced beams so the steel gives us warning bends first!
