1. Wheel Load Factors & Critical Load Placements

Understanding how design axle loads are transformed into cumulative standard axles (102 kN single axle, 180 kN tandem axle) and evaluating Westergaard's three primary stress locations.

1

Corner Stress ($S_c$)

Occurs when the wheel load is applied at the slab corner bisector. Generates cantilever action resulting in highest tensile stress at the TOP surface of the slab.

$S_c = \frac{3P}{h^2}\left[1 - \left(\frac{a\sqrt{2}}{l}\right)^{0.6}\right]$
2

Edge Stress ($S_e$)

CRITICAL

Occurs along the longitudinal edge far from corners. Causes maximum bending deflection, producing highest tensile stress at the BOTTOM surface (governing design criteria).

$S_e = \frac{0.529P}{h^2}(1+0.54\mu)\left[\log_{10}\left(\frac{l}{b}\right)+0.359\right]$
3

Interior Stress ($S_i$)

Wheel load is applied deep within the slab interior. The load is supported symmetrically in all directions, yielding the lowest overall stress magnitude.

$S_i = \frac{0.316P}{h^2}\left[4\log_{10}\left(\frac{l}{b}\right)+1.069\right]$

2. Environmental & Temperature Factors

Concrete slabs experience severe daily thermal gradients causing bending (warping) and seasonal temperature drops causing overall contraction resisted by subgrade friction.

Select Thermal Condition:
Top is hotter than bottom $\rightarrow$ Edges curl downward

Cross-Sectional Slab Warping Profile

Thermal Mechanics Details

Daytime Gradient: Solar radiation heats the slab surface ($T_{top} > T_{bottom}$).

The top expands more than the bottom, causing the slab to **curl downward** at the edges. Self-weight prevents full curling, inducing **high tensile stress at the bottom surface**.

Critical Combination:
Max Stress = Wheel Edge Stress + Daytime Edge Warping Stress

4. Subgrade Support & Dry Lean Concrete (DLC) Sub-base

Evaluating foundation stiffness via Modulus of Subgrade Reaction ($k$) from Plate Load Tests and understanding how Dry Lean Concrete (DLC) prevents subgrade soil pumping.

Plate Load Test (75 cm Diameter)

Subgrade stiffness ($k$) is measured by pressing a standard 75 cm diameter steel plate into the subgrade to cause a standard displacement ($\delta = 1.25$ mm).

$k = \frac{p}{\delta} = \frac{\text{Pressure for } 1.25\text{ mm deflection}}{0.125\text{ cm}}$ (kg/cm³)
Natural Clay Soil Subgrade: 2.5 - 4.5 kg/cm³
Granular Sub-base (GSB): 5.5 - 8.0 kg/cm³
With Dry Lean Concrete (DLC): 15.0 - 30.0 kg/cm³

Subgrade Pumping Phenomenon

Under heavy load repetition + free water beneath joints, weak subgrade fine soil converts into mud slurry and gets ejected upward through joint deflections, creating voids beneath slabs!

Solution: Dry Lean Concrete (DLC) Layer

A 100-150mm low-slump zero-slump concrete layer provides an impermeable, erosion-resistant non-erodible working platform.

5. Joint Design & Load Transfer Mechanics

Rigid pavements use transverse joints with steel Dowel Bars for wheel load transfer without faulting, and longitudinal joints with Tie Bars to prevent lateral separation.

Transverse Dowel Bars

Smooth Round Bars

Smooth steel bars placed at transverse contraction/expansion joints. They allow longitudinal thermal expansion/contraction while transferring vertical shear loads across adjacent slabs.

  • Typical Diameters: 25 mm to 38 mm
  • Standard Length: 450 mm @ 300 mm spacing
  • Coated with lubricant on one half to allow free sliding

Longitudinal Tie Bars

Deformed HYSD Bars

Deformed high-yield steel bars installed across longitudinal joints between adjacent traffic lanes. They tie slabs together to prevent joint opening due to lateral sliding.

  • Typical Diameters: 12 mm to 16 mm
  • Bonded firmly to concrete on both ends (NOT for load transfer)
  • Designed to resist total frictional drag force
