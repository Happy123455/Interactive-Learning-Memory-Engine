Rigid Pavement Design & Westergaard Theory

1. Westergaard's Formulation

Westergaard's analysis treats a concrete slab as a homogeneous, elastic, isotropic plate resting on a dense liquid subgrade. The maximum edge stress ($\sigma_e$) caused by a loaded circular tire area is computed by:

σe = 0.572 P/h2 [ 4 log10(ℓ/b) + log10(b) + 0.359 ]

Where:

  • P: Applied Wheel Load (N)
  • h: Slab thickness (mm)
  • ℓ: Radius of relative stiffness = ∜[E·h³ / (12(1-μ²)k)]
  • b: Equivalent radius of resisting section

2. Pavement Structural Types

  • JPCP: Joints every 4-5m. Relies on smooth metal dowel bars to transfer shear forces across contraction joints without letting slabs lock vertically.
  • JRCP: Uses structural steel mesh. Designed to expand spacing of joints to 10-15m, with reinforcement holding shrinkage microcracks tight.
  • CRCP: No expansion joints. Continuous rebars encourage thin hairline crack spacing at 1-2m, giving premium durability under intense transit loading.
  • PCP: Pre-stressed cable steel blocks tensile strain, allowing thinner configurations for runway taxiways and marine ports.

3. Dynamic Load Transfer Efficiency (LTE)

Calculated dynamically as the percentage of load transferred to adjacent slab modules. High LTE prevents joint stepping, pumping, and eventual structural fatigue.
