**Simulation Blueprint: The "Steel-Joint Playground"**

**Objective:** Build a gamified, physics-based simulation of a double-cover butt joint. User calculates "Joint Efficiency" through drag-and-drop mechanics in a high-fidelity, dark-themed cyber dashboard.

**1. Core Environment:**
*   **Aesthetics:** Dark-mode grid (100vh), neon cyan/purple accents, glassmorphism panels.
*   **Interaction:** Pointer-tracking for "Hand Gesture" control. Sweeping the mouse left-to-right oscillates a "Load Tension" force vector on the joint, with real-time screen vibration and high-pitched synth tones increasing as the joint nears "Yield Capacity."

**2. Simulation Mechanics:**
*   **The Joint Canvas:** A central interactive assembly area showing two steel plates meeting at a butt joint with two cover plates. 
*   **Dynamic Components:** Users drag "Bolt" chips from a peripheral menu into the joint holes. Each bolt "snaps" with a metallic chime.
*   **Physics Loop:** As the user sweeps the mouse (tension load), display **Real-time Force Vectors** (F_t, F_s, F_b) animating in green. If load exceeds capacity, the joint visually "deforms" (plastic flow effect) and turns red.

**3. Pedagogy & ELI5 Narration:**
*   **Analogies:** Use the Web Speech API to explain: "Think of this butt joint like two hands holding a rope; the bolts are your fingers—if you pull too hard, your grip slips!"
*   **Visual Aid:** Display a bouncy, cartoon-style **"Efficiency Bar"**. As users adjust plate thickness (t) and bolt diameter (d) using sliders, the bar animates and "cheers" (reward sound) when efficiency hits optimal design targets.

**4. Advanced UI/UX:**
*   **Formula Slots:** Provide drop-zones for "Bolt Diameter" and "Plate Thickness" variables. When correct cards are dropped into the `Efficiency = (P_joint / P_solid) * 100` formula slot, the numbers glow, and an animation draws the structural failure plane.
*   **IS 456/800 References:** A floating "Codebook" widget; clicking pages (e.g., Pg 73) triggers a pop-up highlighting Clause 10.2 constraints, requiring the user to verify bolt spacing against their current assembly.
